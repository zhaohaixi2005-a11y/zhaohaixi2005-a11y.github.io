Research Question

How can modeling connect molecular interactions with growth dynamics to guide synthetic biology design? This project developed the TriScale digital twin workflow to address that question.

Motivation

Experimental iteration in synthetic biology can be costly. Modeling can reduce trial-and-error testing and support better engineering decisions.

Approach

  • Integrated molecular docking, AI-guided enzyme redesign, and machine learning models of growth curves.
  • Predicted binding affinities between PEX5 and PTS1 sequences to guide targeting-signal selection.
  • Redesigned the rate-limiting enzyme t-HMGR to improve predicted catalytic stability.
  • Modeled Yarrowia lipolytica growth with Gompertz fits and Random Forest/XGBoost models.

My Contributions

  • Led the design of the modeling workflow and the integration of its submodules.
  • Connected predictions of molecular interactions with optimization of the overall process.
  • Established a quantitative framework for the team’s engineering decisions.

Progress and Outputs

  • Reduced trial-and-error testing during pathway and condition optimization.
  • Developed a coherent design rationale grounded in the models for the competition materials.
  • Contributed to Gold Medal and Best iGemers recognition.

Methods and Tools

  • Molecular docking pipelines, machine learning, and growth-curve fitting
  • Technical leadership across subteams and interpretation of model results
  • Using model predictions to guide synthetic biology experiments
