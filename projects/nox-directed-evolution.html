Research Project 04

NOX Engineering for NAD⁺ Regeneration and H₂O₂ Supply

Structure-guided engineering and directed evolution of NADH oxidases, targeting FAD retention and controlled H₂O₂ supply for lignin-degradation cascades.

Mar 2024 - Present Research Project

Academic Guidance

Institution HUST
Supervisor
Prof. Hongbo Yu
NAD⁺ Regeneration Molecular Docking MD Simulation Directed Evolution

Research Question

Can engineering the NOX–FAD interface improve cofactor retention and catalytic performance, supporting NAD⁺ regeneration and H₂O₂ supply in a coupled lignin-degradation system?

Motivation

H₂O₂-producing NADH oxidases oxidize NADH to NAD⁺ while reducing oxygen to peroxide. Coupling this reaction to lignin degradation requires attention to enzyme activity, cofactor interactions, and peroxide supply.

Approach

  • Screen NOX isoforms and reaction systems for NAD⁺ regeneration and H₂O₂ co-production.
  • Combine MEGA-X sequence alignment, docking, and thermodynamic interaction modeling to prioritize residues involved in NADH and FAD recognition.
  • Use molecular dynamics, saturation mutagenesis, and directed evolution to investigate and remodel the NOX–FAD interface.
  • Evaluate candidate variants with the aim of improving catalytic efficiency and H₂O₂ output for lignin-degradation systems.

My Contributions

  • Screened NOX isoforms and reaction systems for a coupled NAD⁺-regeneration and peroxide-supply workflow.
  • Integrated sequence, docking, and interaction-modeling evidence to prioritize substrate- and cofactor-recognition residues.
  • Applied molecular dynamics and mutation strategies to study FAD retention and prioritize engineering candidates.
