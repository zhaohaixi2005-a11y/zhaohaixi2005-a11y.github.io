Research Project 04

NADH Oxidase Engineering for NAD⁺ Regeneration and H₂O₂ Supply

This project combines structure-guided engineering and directed evolution of NADH oxidases (NOX), with the aim of improving FAD retention and controlling H₂O₂ supply in lignin-degradation cascades.

Research Question

Can engineering the NOX–FAD interface improve cofactor retention and catalytic performance to support NAD⁺ regeneration and H₂O₂ supply in a coupled lignin-degradation system?

Motivation

H₂O₂-producing NADH oxidases oxidize NADH to NAD⁺ while reducing oxygen to peroxide. To couple this reaction with lignin degradation, enzyme activity, cofactor interactions, and peroxide supply must be considered together.

Approach

  • Screen NOX isoforms and reaction systems for simultaneous NAD⁺ regeneration and H₂O₂ production.
  • Combine MEGA-X sequence alignment, docking, and thermodynamic interaction modeling to prioritize residues involved in NADH and FAD recognition.
  • Use molecular dynamics, saturation mutagenesis, and directed evolution to investigate and remodel the NOX–FAD interface.
  • Evaluate candidate variants for potential improvements in catalytic efficiency and H₂O₂ production in lignin-degradation systems.

My Contributions

  • Screened NOX isoforms and reaction systems for a workflow that couples NAD⁺ regeneration with peroxide supply.
  • Integrated sequence analysis, docking, and interaction models to prioritize residues involved in substrate and cofactor recognition.
  • Applied molecular dynamics and mutation strategies to study FAD retention and prioritize engineering candidates.

Progress and Publications
