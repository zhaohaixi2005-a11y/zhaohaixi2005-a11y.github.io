Research Project 05

Profiling Hypoxia Responses in Colonic Organoids

This project combined microfluidic perturbations with single-cell analysis to map hypoxia responses and characterize cell states in colonic organoids.

Research Question

How do hypoxia and nutrient deprivation change the cell-state composition of colonic organoids? This project investigated those responses using a microfluidic platform that simulates pathological conditions.

Motivation

Mapping how cells adapt to stress can clarify how changes in the microenvironment alter cellular phenotypes.

Approach

  • Refined PCA and UMAP dimensionality reduction and marker-based annotation to improve cluster resolution and identify distinct cell subpopulations.
  • Tuned mitochondrial-content thresholds during scRNA-seq quality control to retain stress-responsive cells and improve detection of hypoxia-associated transcriptional signatures.
  • Optimized the preprocessing pipeline to improve subpopulation annotation.
  • Analyzed differentially expressed genes and stress-response programs to characterize cell-state changes induced by hypoxia and nutrient deprivation.

My Contributions

  • Refined dimensionality reduction and quality-control parameters to recover biologically relevant signals.
  • Refined marker-based annotation to identify distinct cell subpopulations.
  • Analyzed differentially expressed genes and stress-response programs in microfluidic organoid models.

Progress and Outputs

  • Improved resolution of hypoxia-associated cell subpopulations.
  • Improved the integrated workflow for interpreting single-cell RNA-seq data.
  • Developed a structured analysis workflow for future organoid perturbation studies.

Methods and Tools

  • Scanpy for single-cell quality control, annotation, and dimensionality reduction diagnostics
  • Interpreting transcriptomic data in the context of microfluidic perturbations
  • Statistical interpretation of condition-specific transcriptomic changes
