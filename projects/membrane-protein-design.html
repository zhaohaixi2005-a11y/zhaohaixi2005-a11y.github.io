Research Question

How can bridge architectures support distinct conformational states in de novo membrane proteins while balancing the flexibility required for conformational changes with structural stability?

Motivation

Designing proteins for different conformational states requires a distinct starting structure for each state. When de novo structure predictions converge on similar conformations, template-guided prediction can provide alternative starting structures for designing connecting helices, domains, and bridges.

Approach

  • Benchmark AlphaFold 2.3, AlphaFold 3, Boltz-2, and Chai-1 in template-free and template-guided settings.
  • Use template-guided AlphaFold 3 to recover distinct predicted open and closed conformations as starting structures for state-specific design.
  • Apply RFdiffusion to transmembrane helices, soluble domains, and bridge architectures, tuning guiding potentials and filtering for predicted stability and lipid compatibility.
  • Perform molecular cloning, protein expression and purification, and cryo-EM sample preparation for structural validation of selected designs.

My Contributions

  • Benchmarked structure prediction methods and template settings to establish starting structures for each conformational state.
  • Applied RFdiffusion to design helices, domains, and bridges; tuned guiding potentials and filtered candidate designs.
  • Performed molecular cloning, protein expression and purification, and cryo-EM sample preparation.

Progress and Outputs

  • Template-guided AlphaFold 3 produced distinct predicted open and closed structures for subsequent bridge design.
  • Selected designs progressed through molecular cloning, protein expression and purification, and cryo-EM sample preparation.

Methods and Tools

  • Template-guided structure prediction and analysis of lipid compatibility
