Research Question

How can bridge architectures support distinct conformational states in de novo membrane proteins while balancing the flexibility needed for motion with structural stability?

Motivation

State-specific design first requires distinct structural starting points. When de novo predictions collapse into a similar conformation, template-guided prediction can provide alternative seeds for designing connecting helices, domains, and bridges.

Approach

  • Benchmark AlphaFold2.3, AlphaFold3, Boltz-2, and Chai-1 in template-free and template-guided settings.
  • Use template-guided AlphaFold3 to recover distinct computational open and closed conformations as state-specific design seeds.
  • Apply RFdiffusion to transmembrane helices, soluble domains, and bridge architectures, tuning guiding potentials and filtering for predicted stability and lipid compatibility.
  • Perform cloning, protein expression and purification, and cryo-EM sample preparation toward structural validation of selected designs.

My Contributions

  • Benchmarked prediction methods and template settings to establish state-specific structural starting points.
  • Applied RFdiffusion to helix, domain, and bridge design, with guiding-potential tuning and candidate filtering.
  • Performed molecular cloning, expression, purification, and cryo-EM sample preparation.

Current Progress

  • Template-guided AlphaFold3 produced distinct computational open and closed seeds for subsequent bridge design.
  • Selected designs advanced through cloning, expression, purification, and cryo-EM preparation.

Tools
