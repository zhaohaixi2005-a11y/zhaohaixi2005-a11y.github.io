Research Project 06

tRNA Modification and Translational Regulation in Cancer

This project used structural modeling to connect NSUN2- and NAT10-mediated tRNA modifications with codon-biased translation, oncogenic protein production, and protein stability.

Research Question

How do tRNA modifications alter oncogenic translation and protein stability in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC)?

Motivation

tRNA modifications can bias translation efficiency and change the production of cancer-associated proteins. Understanding the links between modification enzymes, tRNA substrates, and downstream protein stability requires structural and molecular evidence.

Approach

  • Analyzed data on codon usage and translation efficiency for the NSUN2–tRNAVal-CAC and NAT10–tRNA ac4C pathways.
  • Built structural models of NSUN2–tRNAVal-CAC and OTUB1–c-Myc interactions using AlphaFold 3.
  • Combined molecular docking and interface analysis to interpret regulatory mechanisms associated with tRNA modification.
  • Integrated structural and molecular evidence into mechanistic models of TNBC and ATC progression.

My Contributions

  • Performed structural modeling and analyzed interfaces in enzyme–tRNA and protein–protein systems.
  • Related codon usage to translation efficiency and patterns of oncogenic protein production.
  • Helped integrate computational evidence supporting a positive feedback loop between NAT10 and c-Myc.

Progress and Outputs

  • Supported mechanistic interpretation of NSUN2-driven codon-biased translation in TNBC.
  • Integrated structural and molecular evidence to support a mechanistic model linking NAT10-mediated tRNA ac4C regulation, OTUB1-dependent c-Myc stabilization, and ATC progression.
  • Contributed to two publications in the SCI-indexed journal Cellular & Molecular Biology Letters.

Methods and Tools

  • AlphaFold 3, molecular docking, protein interface analysis
  • Analysis of codon usage and translation efficiency
  • Integrating evidence from structural biology and cancer biology
