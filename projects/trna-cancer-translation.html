tRNA Modification-Mediated Translational Regulation in Cancer

A structure-informed cancer biology project linking NSUN2- and NAT10-mediated tRNA modification axes to codon-biased translation, oncogenic protein output, and protein stability.

Project Overview

This project investigated how tRNA modification pathways reshape oncogenic translation programs and protein stabilization mechanisms in triple-negative breast cancer and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma.

Research Motivation

tRNA modification can bias translation efficiency and alter cancer-associated protein output, but the structural and molecular links between modification enzymes, tRNA substrates, and downstream protein stability require integrated analysis.

Methods / Pipeline

  • Analyzed codon usage and translation efficiency data for NSUN2-tRNA Val-CAC and NAT10-tRNA ac4C axes.
  • Built AlphaFold3-based structural models for NSUN2-tRNA Val-CAC and OTUB1-c-Myc interactions.
  • Combined molecular docking and interface analysis to interpret modification-linked regulatory mechanisms.
  • Integrated structural and molecular evidence into mechanistic models for TNBC and ATC progression.

My Contributions

  • Performed structure-based modeling and interface interpretation for enzyme-tRNA and protein-protein systems.
  • Connected codon-usage analysis with translation-efficiency and oncogenic output patterns.
  • Helped synthesize computational evidence supporting the NAT10-c-Myc positive feedback mechanism.

Key Outcomes

  • Supported mechanistic interpretation of NSUN2-driven codon-biased translation in TNBC.
  • Modeled the NAT10-tRNA ac4C and OTUB1-c-Myc regulatory axis in ATC progression.
  • Contributed to two SCI publications in Cellular & Molecular Biology Letters.

Related Tools / Skills

  • AlphaFold3, molecular docking, protein interface analysis
  • Codon-usage and translation-efficiency interpretation
  • Mechanistic synthesis across structural biology and cancer biology
