Constrained Design of Protein Assemblies

Research Question

How can a generative model build a coherent protein assembly while preserving specified motifs and interfaces, creating new contacts, and maintaining connectivity and global symmetry?

Motivation

In assembly design, local structural constraints must be compatible with the overall architecture. This workflow encodes those constraints in a programmable specification and enforces them throughout diffusion sampling.

Approach

  • Compile declarative specifications of components, motifs, interface roles, connectivity, symmetry, and topology into executable RFD3 conditioning programs.
  • Represent residues and geometric constraints through symmetry-coupled orbits and constraint groups, maintaining their relationships throughout sampling.
  • Use group-constrained SE(3) transformations to reposition mobile or partially constrained components while keeping fixed components in place and preserving internal geometry.
  • Combine denoising of local neighborhoods with geometric constraints to scaffold motifs and build assemblies containing both preserved and newly generated interfaces.

My Contributions

  • Developed the assembly task specification, compiler, and constrained sampling workflow.
  • Implemented methods to propagate constraints on motifs, interfaces, symmetry, and higher-order geometry through residue orbits and constraint groups.
  • Introduced group-constrained mobility and denoising of local neighborhoods to couple scaffold generation with component placement.

Progress and Outputs

  • The framework covers de novo oligomer generation, motif- and interface-conditioned scaffolding, and multi-interface tasks.
  • Supported symmetries include cyclic, dihedral, polyhedral, and helical arrangements for rings, cages, and higher-order assemblies.
  • Ongoing computational evaluation focuses on preserving geometry and symmetry while generating new scaffolds and interfaces.
