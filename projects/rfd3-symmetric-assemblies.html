Research Project 01

Constrained Design of Protein Assemblies

I am developing RFD3-Mosaic, an RFdiffusion3-based workflow for designing protein assemblies with specified interfaces, connectivity, and symmetry.

Research Question

How can a generative model build a coherent protein assembly while preserving supplied motifs and interfaces, creating new contacts, and respecting connectivity and global symmetry?

Motivation

Assembly design requires local structural requirements to remain compatible with the overall architecture. Mosaic makes these requirements explicit in a programmable specification and carries them into the diffusion trajectory.

Approach

  • Compile declarative components, motifs, interface roles, connectivity, symmetry, and topology into executable RFD3 conditioning programs.
  • Represent residues and geometric constraints through symmetry-coupled orbits and constraint groups, maintaining their relationships throughout sampling.
  • Use group-constrained SE(3) mobility to let fixed, mobile, or partially constrained components adapt while preserving internal geometry.
  • Combine local-neighborhood denoising with constraints for motif scaffolding and assemblies containing both preserved and newly generated interfaces.

My Contributions

  • Developed the assembly task specification, compiler, and constrained sampling workflow.
  • Implemented propagation of motifs, interfaces, symmetry, and higher-order geometric relationships through residue orbits and constraint groups.
  • Introduced group-constrained mobility and local-neighborhood denoising to couple scaffold generation with component placement.

Current Progress

  • The framework covers de novo oligomer generation, motif- and interface-conditioned scaffolding, and multi-interface tasks.
  • Its supported symmetry scope includes cyclic, dihedral, polyhedral, and helical architectures for rings, cages, and higher-order assemblies.
  • Ongoing computational evaluation focuses on preserving geometry and symmetry while generating new scaffolds and interfaces.
