Research Question

How can protein assembly specifications be compiled into diffusion sampling algorithms that preserve structural constraints while generating new scaffolds and interfaces?

Motivation

Protein assembly tasks combine requirements at several scales: motifs and interface geometry, component connectivity, symmetry, and topology. The methodological challenge is to represent these requirements together and translate them into sampling rules that maintain the specified relationships as new structures are generated.

Approach

  • Compile declarative specifications of components, motifs, interface roles, connectivity, symmetry, and topology into executable RFD3 conditioning programs.
  • Formulate backbone diffusion in terms of symmetry-coupled residue orbits and constraint groups, propagating geometric relationships throughout sampling.
  • Use group-constrained SE(3) transformations to reposition mobile or partially constrained components while keeping fixed components in place and preserving internal geometry.
  • Combine denoising of local neighborhoods with geometric constraints to scaffold motifs and build assemblies containing both preserved and newly generated interfaces.

My Contributions

  • Developed a declarative representation of protein assembly tasks and a compiler that turns structural requirements into executable RFD3 conditioning programs.
  • Reformulated diffusion sampling using symmetry-coupled residue orbits and constraint groups to propagate motifs, interfaces, and higher-order geometric constraints.
  • Introduced group-constrained SE(3) mobility and local-neighborhood denoising to couple component placement with scaffold generation.

Progress and Outputs

  • The framework covers de novo oligomer generation, motif- and interface-conditioned scaffolding, and multi-interface tasks.
  • Supported symmetries include cyclic, dihedral, polyhedral, and helical arrangements for rings, cages, and higher-order assemblies.
  • Ongoing computational evaluation focuses on preserving geometry and symmetry while generating new scaffolds and interfaces.
